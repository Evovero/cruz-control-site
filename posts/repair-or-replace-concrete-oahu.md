<p>If you're staring at a cracked driveway or an uneven patio slab, the first question is always the same: patch it or pour new? For most Oahu homeowners, the answer depends on how deep the damage goes and what's causing it, not just how it looks on the surface. After 12+ years pouring and repairing concrete across Waianae, Kapolei, Ewa Beach, and the rest of Oahu, we've learned to read a slab before we ever touch it.</p>

<h2>What Actually Causes Concrete to Fail on Oahu</h2>
<p>Concrete here takes a different kind of beating than concrete on the mainland. Salt air corrodes exposed rebar faster near the coast in places like Ko Olina and Hawaii Kai. Expansive soil in older neighborhoods shifts with our wet and dry seasons, which is what causes the slow, uneven settling homeowners notice as one corner of a slab sinking while the rest stays put. And heavy seasonal rain finds any weak point in drainage and works it wider every year. None of that means every crack is an emergency, but it does mean the cause matters more than the crack itself when deciding what to do next.</p>

<h2>Signs a Repair Is Still the Right Call</h2>
<p>Hairline surface cracks that haven't widened, minor spalling on the top layer, and small settling of an inch or less are usually repairable. So is a driveway with cosmetic damage but a solid structural base underneath. We check this by looking at whether the slab is still level and whether the cracking is isolated or spreading. A patio in Maili or Nanakuli with a few surface cracks from age, rather than from a failing base, is a good repair candidate. Repairing what's still structurally sound is almost always the right call before jumping to a full tear-out.</p>

<h2>Signs It's Time to Replace</h2>
<p>Some damage tells you a patch won't hold. Multiple cracks running through the full depth of the slab, sections that have sunk more than an inch or two, or a driveway that rocks or flexes under a vehicle usually means the base underneath has failed, not just the surface. When we see that on a job in Aiea, Makaha, or Kahala, we're honest that a repair would be a short-term fix on a slab that's going to keep moving. Replacement lets us rebuild the base properly, with the right compaction and drainage, so the new pour doesn't inherit the same problem in a few years.</p>

<h2>What a Proper Repair Looks Like</h2>
<p>A repair worth doing addresses the cause, not just the crack. That means cleaning out and sealing surface cracks so water stops getting underneath, mudjacking or foam leveling a slab that's settled slightly, and resurfacing areas with cosmetic spalling. Done right, a repair on a driveway or patio should hold up for years, not just look better for one season before the same crack reopens.</p>

<h2>What a Proper Replacement Looks Like</h2>
<p>A full replacement starts below the surface. We remove the old slab, address whatever caused it to fail (usually drainage or a soft, poorly compacted base), and rebuild with proper compaction, reinforcement, and control joints placed to manage the natural expansion and contraction Hawaii's climate puts concrete through. A new <a href="/concrete-driveway-contractor/">driveway</a>, <a href="/concrete-patio-contractor/">patio</a>, or <a href="/concrete-slab-contractor/">slab</a> built this way is designed to handle the same salt air and soil conditions that took out the old one.</p>

<h2>Getting an Honest Answer for Your Property</h2>
<p>The only real way to know is to have someone walk the slab and check what's happening underneath the cracks, not just the cracks themselves. We look at the base, the drainage, and how the damage has progressed before we ever recommend one path over the other. If a repair will genuinely hold, we say so. If it won't, we'd rather tell a homeowner in Kahala or Hawaii Kai up front than sell a patch job that fails again in a year.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>How can I tell if my driveway needs repair or replacement?</h3>
<p>Look at whether cracks are widening, whether any section has sunk noticeably, and whether the surface flexes or rocks under weight. Isolated, stable surface damage usually means repair. Spreading cracks or an unstable base usually mean replacement.</p>

<h3>Why does concrete crack faster near the coast on Oahu?</h3>
<p>Salt air accelerates corrosion of the rebar reinforcement inside concrete, and that corrosion expands and cracks the surrounding concrete from the inside out. Properties in Ko Olina, Hawaii Kai, and other coastal areas see this pattern more than inland neighborhoods.</p>

<h3>Can a settled slab be fixed without a full replacement?</h3>
<p>Often yes, through mudjacking or foam leveling, as long as the slab itself is still structurally sound and the settling is minor. If the base has failed entirely, leveling the existing slab just delays a replacement rather than solving the problem.</p>

<h3>How long should a new driveway or patio last on Oahu?</h3>
<p>A properly built slab, with the right base preparation and control joints for our climate, should hold up for decades. Most early failures we see trace back to inadequate base compaction or drainage during the original pour, not the concrete itself.</p>

<h3>Is patching a crack myself a bad idea?</h3>
<p>A DIY patch can buy time on a purely cosmetic crack, but it won't stop water intrusion or address a failing base the way a proper repair does, and it can make the eventual professional repair more involved if it's covering up a bigger issue.</p>
