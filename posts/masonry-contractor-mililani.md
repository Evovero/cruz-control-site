<h2>Why Mililani Properties Often Need Retaining Walls</h2>
<p>We see a lot of Mililani lots with a grade change between the street, the driveway, and the backyard. A retaining wall holds that grade in place, whether it is a low wall separating a lawn from a patio or a taller wall supporting a driveway cut into a slope.</p>
<p>We build retaining walls using concrete block, poured concrete, and natural stone veneer, matched to the look of the home and the load the wall needs to carry. A wall holding back a few feet of soil under a garden bed is a different engineering problem than one supporting a driveway or a section of yard above a lower neighbor's property line.</p>

<h2>Masonry Work Beyond Retaining Walls</h2>
<p>Masonry contractor work in Mililani covers more than retaining walls. Block walls for property lines and privacy, planter boxes, mailbox columns, and outdoor kitchen bases all fall under the same trade. Concrete is a <a href="https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/termite-resistant-foundations-and-walls">termite resistant building material</a>, which matters on Oahu.</p>
<p>Repair and restoration work makes up a steady share of our masonry calls too. Older block walls can <a href="https://www.cmha.org/resource/tek-08-01a/">develop cracked mortar joints</a>, and less often, spalled block. <a href="https://www.cmha.org/resource/tek-08-01a/">Catching these issues early</a> is usually a smaller job than waiting until a wall shifts enough to need a full rebuild.</p>

<h2>Working With Oahu's Soil and Climate</h2>
<p><a href="https://www3.ctahr.hawaii.edu/oc/freepubs/pdf/soils_poster_3.pdf">Soil conditions vary across Oahu</a>, and they affect how a retaining wall or masonry footing needs to be built. Proper drainage behind a retaining wall is not optional here. Without weep holes, gravel backfill, and a drainage layer, <a href="https://www.cmha.org/resource/tek-15-07b/">water can build up behind the wall</a> after heavy rain and push it out of position over time.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.weather.gov/hfo/climate_summary">Rainfall in Hawaii can change</a> a lot over short distances, and the heaviest rains come between October and April, so we plan drainage for each site. We build every wall with drainage and curing conditions in mind rather than treating masonry the same way it might get built on the mainland.</p>

<h2>Retaining Wall Height and Design Considerations</h2>
<p>Wall height changes what goes into a project. A low decorative wall separating a garden bed from a lawn is mostly about appearance and light erosion control. A taller wall holding back several feet of soil under a driveway or supporting a slope near a home's foundation is a structural project that needs proper engineering, footing depth, and reinforcement.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Low walls (under 2 feet):</strong> Often decorative or minor grade control. Honolulu DPP <a href="https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/building-permits-home/building-permit-requriements/">does not require a building permit</a> for retaining walls 30 inches or shorter, but the work still has to follow city rules.</li>
<li><strong>Mid-height walls:</strong> Honolulu DPP <a href="https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/building-permits-home/building-permit-requriements/">requires a building permit</a> for a retaining wall over 30 inches tall. These walls also need drainage behind them.</li>
<li><strong>Tall or load-bearing walls:</strong> Honolulu DPP requires <a href="https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/building-permits-home/building-permit-requriements/">plans stamped by an architect or engineer</a> for retaining walls 5 feet or taller.</li>
</ul>
<p>We walk every Mililani property before recommending a wall type, since the right approach depends on the slope, what the wall needs to hold, and how it fits with the rest of the yard.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>How do I know if I need a retaining wall in Mililani?</h3>
<p>If your property has a noticeable grade change between the street, driveway, or yard, or if you are seeing soil erosion after rain, a retaining wall is likely worth considering. We assess the slope and drainage on site before recommending wall height or materials.</p>

<h3>What materials are used for retaining walls on Oahu?</h3>
<p>Common options include concrete block, poured concrete, and natural stone veneer. The right material depends on the wall's height, the load it needs to support, and how it should look next to the rest of the home's exterior.</p>

<h3>Do retaining walls need drainage systems?</h3>
<p>Yes. Water pressure builds up behind a retaining wall without proper drainage, and <a href="https://web.mst.edu/rogersda/umrcourses/ge441/online_lectures/subsurface_drainage/GE441-Lecture5-5.pdf">poor drainage is one of the most common reasons</a> walls fail. Every properly built retaining wall needs <a href="https://www.cmha.org/resource/tek-15-07b/">a way to keep water from building up</a> behind it. We plan the drainage for each wall.</p>

<h3>Can an existing block wall be repaired instead of replaced?</h3>
<p>Often, yes. Cracks in the mortar joints can usually be <a href="https://www.cmha.org/resource/tek-08-01a/">repaired by repointing</a>, and damaged block should be repaired before it gets worse. We look at the wall in person and tell you whether it can be repaired or needs a rebuild.</p>

<h3>Do retaining walls require permits in Mililani?</h3>
<p>Honolulu DPP <a href="https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/building-permits-home/building-permit-requriements/">requires a building permit</a> for a retaining wall over 30 inches tall, and walls 5 feet or taller need plans stamped by an architect or engineer. We confirm what your project needs with DPP during planning. We can walk through what a specific project needs during a site visit.</p>

<h3>How long does a retaining wall last on Oahu?</h3>
<p><a href="https://www.nachi.org/life-expectancy.htm">Home inspector estimates</a> put concrete block at 100 years or more. How long a retaining wall lasts depends a lot on its drainage. <a href="https://web.mst.edu/rogersda/umrcourses/ge441/online_lectures/subsurface_drainage/GE441-Lecture5-5.pdf">Poor drainage is one of the most common reasons</a> retaining walls fail.</p>
